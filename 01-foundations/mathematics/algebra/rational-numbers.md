# Rational Numbers
A rational number is any number that can be expressed as a fraction (p/q), where:
1. p and q are integers (...,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3...)
2. q!=0(denominator cannot be zero)

Examples:
  - Positive fractions: 1/2, 3/4, 7/5
  - Negative fractions: -2/3, -5/7
  - Integers (special case of fractions): 2 = 2/1, -3 = -3/1
  - Decimal numbers that terminate or repeat: 0.35 = 1/4, 0.333... = 1/3

## Properties of rational numbers
- Closed under addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division (except division by 0)
- Can be written as either fractions or decimals (terminating or repeating)
- The set of rational numbers is dense, meaning between any two rational numbers, ther is
  always another rational number.

## Key insight
- All integers are rational numbers, but not all rational numbers are integers.
- Rational numbers contrast with irrational numbers, which cannot be expressed as fractions.

## Fun Fact
Any rational number can be expressed in "lowest terms", where the numerator and denominator
share no common divisors other than 1
