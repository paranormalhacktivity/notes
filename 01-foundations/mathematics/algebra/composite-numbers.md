# Composite Numbers
A composite number is a natural number greater than 1 that has more than two positive divisors.
In other words, it is not prime. Composite numbers can be factored into smaller natural numbers
beside 1 and itself.

Examples: 4,6,8,9,12...

## Properties of Composite Numbers
- Divisible by numbers other than 1 and itself.
- All even numbers > 2 are composeite, because they're divisible by 2.
- Can be expressed as a product of prime numbers (prime factorization).
- Used to identify relationships with primes, like LCM, GCD, and factoring problems.

## Prime Factorization Example
Number: 18
Prime Factorization: 18 = 2 * 3^2
18 is composite because it has divisors other than 1 and itself: 2,3,6,9

## Fun Fact
The numbers 1 and 0, while being considered natural numbers are neither prime nor composite.
However, excluding those two special cases, composite numbers are like "opposite sides"
of primes, therefore combining composite and prime sets, make up the complete structure of
natural numbers.
